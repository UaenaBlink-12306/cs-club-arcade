# Soccer rules and input audit

Audited against the IFAB 2026/27 Laws on 2026-11-11. This remains a stylised football game, **not full IFAB compliance**. The source of truth is `public/soccer-skills/index.html`; `soccer-skills/index.html` mirrors it.

## Implemented rules

- **Kick-off:** explicit `setPiece='Kick-off'` and restart guard. The ball stays on the centre mark and the taker cannot dribble. Other players remain in their own half, and opponents outside the centre circle, until contact. Apply at start, second half and after every goal; the conceding team restarts. Mouse/touch aim and release, solo E/Space and each local player's pass/shot keys work. Solo opposition takes its own AI kick; a human-controlled taker never auto-kicks. The opening match clock starts with the kick. [Law 8](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-start-and-restart-of-play/)
- **Goal kicks:** ball in the goal area; every opponent is placed outside the complete penalty area and constrained there through wind-up. Teammates may stay and receive inside. The restriction ends at contact/launch, without waiting for the ball to leave the area. Early entry is prevented by positioning correction before interaction or contact, including human input. There is no blanket ten-yard radius. [Law 16](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-goal-kick/)
- **Corners:** nearest corner, ball within its arc, opponents held at least the scaled required distance from the arc until contact. [Law 17](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-corner-kick/)
- **Throw-ins:** correct last-touch ownership, crossing location, taker on the touchline, inward delivery and opponent spacing. A held/overhead pose replaces a foot strike. Direct throws into the opposing goal become goal kicks; direct throws into one's own goal become corners. [Law 15](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-throw-in/)
- **Boundary decisions:** whole-ball crossing, with a shared physical/drawing radius; earliest crossed boundary wins on diagonal exits. Goal mouth and crossbar clearance account for the ball's radius and rendered height scale. [Law 9](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-ball-in-and-out-of-play/), [Law 10](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/determining-the-outcome-of-a-match/)
- **Contact fouls:** fast closing contact from behind when the ball is out of reach, a late collision with the recent passer after the ball has gone, and charging a keeper holding the ball are whistled. Detection uses actual motion and contact geometry, not a random foul roll. Stationary proximity, retreating opponents and ball-side tackles are not automatically offences. The victim and contact location are recorded, a brief reaction pose identifies the player, and the stoppage names the reason. AI defenders approach around a shielded ball to avoid repeatedly charging through players. These are simplified careless challenges; no severity/card classification is claimed. [Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/)
- **Limited shot advantage:** after a careless late challenge, an already-launched shot projected to enter the goal within 1.2 seconds may finish. A goal stands; otherwise the next touch, exit or two-second timeout recalls the stored free kick/penalty. This deliberately narrow trial does not implement general possession advantage or cards. [Law 5](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-referee/)
- **Direct free kicks:** contact fouls outside the offender's penalty area restart at the estimated contact position. Opponents respect the shared free-kick distance; a defending-area free kick also keeps them outside the box. Defenders on their own goal line between the posts may use the close-free-kick exception. The ball remains stationary until the normal kick input; direct opposing goals count, direct own goals do not. No dedicated three-player wall formation is offered. [Law 13](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/free-kicks/)
- **In-match penalties:** contact fouls inside or on the offender's penalty-area line move the ball to the shared penalty mark. The identified taker cannot dribble; the defending keeper stays on the goal line between the posts and can move laterally. Other players stay behind the spot and outside the box and arc until contact. Backward kicks are rejected; forward releases use actual ball flight, keeper saves and goal detection. Local players control their own taker/defending keeper; solo opposition takes its penalty automatically. The usual second-touch restriction remains. A penalty pending at half/full time is completed before the interval/final whistle; an extended-time goal gets its replay but no subsequent kickoff. No stochastic shootout shortcut determines an in-match penalty. [Law 14](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-penalty-kick/)
- **Second touch and indirect free kicks:** retain restart type/team/taker until another player touches. A second touch awards an indirect free kick, including special placement outside the attacking goal area. The taker's off-ball AI does not chase its untouched restart; a human can still incur the offence. Indirect kicks enforce spacing and cannot score directly. Direct own-goal restarts award corners. [Law 13](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/free-kicks/)
- **Offside involvement:** snapshot positions at pass contact against the ball, second-last opponent and halfway line. Penalise reception even after a return onside. Also penalise a close active challenge for a playable ball that affects a nearby opponent, or a clear goalkeeper-view obstruction during a goal-bound shot. These two involvement checks use conservative spatial/motion approximations, not full referee judgement. Mere position or passive proximity is insufficient. Direct goal kicks, corners and throw-ins remain exempt; a later teammate play creates a fresh phase. Controlled opposition possession resets it. Stoppages identify OFFSIDE, the reason and the offence location. [Law 11](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/offside/)
- **Goalkeeper possession:** hands available only in the keeper's own penalty area for an opposing delivery. Teammate deliveries and balls outside the area use ordinary foot control. Hand possession prevents tackling, constrains movement to the area and awards the nearest corner if held over eight seconds. A visible and accessible release countdown covers the final five seconds, clears on release/stoppage and freezes during pause. Foot-controlled deliveries do not start it. [Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/)
- **Shootouts:** existing alternating five-kick sequence, early clinch and equal-attempt sudden death retained; track rotating eligible taker IDs so one player cannot repeat before all eleven have taken a kick. [Law 10](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/determining-the-outcome-of-a-match/)

## Shooting and chance creation

This pass fixes two reproduced shooting failures: a 150-pixel goal-directed drag previously queued a short pass because its endpoint stopped short of goal; and the keeper saved 25 of 27 controlled shots.

- Within 360 field units of goal, a sufficiently powered goal-directed drag now selects a shot by direction. The aiming ray intersects the projected goal line, so drag length controls power rather than whether the endpoint physically reaches the net. This also corrects wide-angle distortion from extending a short ground vector through the angled camera. The preview and release share the same target resolver; it does not clamp aim inside the posts. Explicit teammate targets remain passes.
- A compact SHOT/PASS percentage and power bar show the chosen action. The camera smoothly looks toward the aim during preparation and flight while retaining the contact zoom lock. A short ball trail helps show flight direction.
- Shots use a 140 ms ownership-bound preparation with a longer backswing and a planted support leg. The held ball turns smoothly into the contact position rather than jumping when the request arrives. Passes retain their 75 ms preparation, and maximum shot launch speed remains 550 field units/second.
- AI keepers react after 200 ms rather than immediately reading shot velocity, then move at a bounded speed. Normal hand reach is 14 field units, with a brief 18-unit visible dive window, replacing the previous instant 23-unit catch radius. Human-controlled local keepers remain responsive.
- Fast opposing shots require an outfield defender to reach within 8 units to block; slower ball control retains its existing reach. Swept contact checks prevent fast shots tunnelling through a defender between simulation frames and resolve touches before a later boundary crossing. Saves, misses and actual goal-line crossings remain physical outcomes.
- Receivers get 0.8 seconds of settling protection instead of 0.55. One AI defender presses while a second covers a passing lane. Tackles require closer ball access; an attempt has 0.6 seconds of recovery, including a missed attempt. This reduces immediate repeat steals without removing tackling or interception.

Controlled before/after comparison: the same 27 shots (three ranges, three starting angles and three placements, power 0.85, isolated shooter and live AI keeper) changed from **2 goals / 25 saves** to **12 goals / 15 saves**. This is a scenario comparison, not a human scoring-rate claim. Additional tests cover wide misses, weak long shots being saved, and a powerful accurately placed long-range goal. Full-team input scenarios start at normal kickoff, pass to a teammate, hold a pointer to make an angled run, then release toward the far corner and verify the actual score/replay state.

## Pointer and pass controls

Physical pointer capture now survives possession loss, reception and selection changes. Running retains the held screen direction, mapped again for the new controlled player. A handoff reanchors the kick gesture at the last pointer position and disarms old kick intent. Move/drag again while continuing to hold to aim a fresh pass; simply releasing the old gesture does not shoot. This also handles loss and reacquisition of the same player between frames through the possession serial.

Drag length affects pass power, and a small receiving assist snaps a clearly aimed nearby teammate target. Teammate targets stay passes even near goal. Release queues exactly one ownership-bound kick. Pointer cancellation, capture loss, pause, blur and phase changes clear input safely; secondary pointers cannot steal an active gesture. Simulated mouse and scaled-touch handler sequences cover actual flight to a receiver from kickoff and open play.

## Geometry and deliberate limitations

`RULES` shares pitch marking dimensions, centre-circle distance, penalty/goal areas, corner arc, ball radius and crossbar dimensions with rendering. It uses the existing stylised field; it is not a survey-accurate full-size pitch. Player-centre positions approximate bodies for offside and spacing. A mobile minimum drawing size can exaggerate the ball's visual radius for legibility.

Remaining gaps, **not implemented**:

- Reckless/excessive-force classification, yellow/red cards, general possession advantage, handball, holding, persistent misconduct, off-ball fouls beyond the brief late-challenge window, and substitutions. Current foul detection covers specific careless contact circumstances. Referee judgement is approximated by player-centre/contact-motion thresholds; not every physical offence or disciplinary sanction is modelled.
- Broader offside interference beyond the implemented close-challenge and keeper-view tests; body-part judging, exact simultaneous-offence ordering, off-field player provisions, and deflection/save/rebound distinctions. Close challenges require a playable ball within 26 field units, active approach over 12 units/second and a nearby opponent. Keeper-view checks require an offside body within 60 units of the keeper, within 5 units of the eye-to-ball line, obstructing a goal-bound shot. These are deliberate approximations. There is no separate rebound/parry engine, so deliberate-save and deflection phase preservation is not claimed.
- Quick goal-kick exceptions for opponents unable to leave, referee sanctions for persistent encroachment and timed referee countdowns for delaying goal kicks/throw-ins. Players are placed legally before the restart instead.
- Fully adjudicated throw-in foot/hand faults. The animation enforces a fixed legal delivery location; it does not simulate an illegal throw as an available action.
- Detailed keeper hand-release/drop-kick animation, illegal deliberate handling and accidental double-kick interpretations. Forbidden catches are prevented by choosing foot control, rather than exposed as intentional misconduct.
- Penalty infringement/retake matrices, illegal feints, accidental double kicks, detailed keeper feet/body-part adjudication and separate parry/post-rebound physics. Encroachment is prevented through positioning rather than exposed for referee adjudication. Keeper saves currently catch the ball. In-match penalties have real flight; the separate post-match shootout still uses directional choices, without physical run-ups, coin tosses or eligibility changes.
- Regulation real-time match duration, stoppage-time calculation and full tournament rounds. Cup play remains QF → SF → final; practice remains untimed.

## Near-goal camera framing

Normal gameplay anticipates the attacking goal as the ball approaches shooting range (360 world units, fully engaged by 240). It fits the carrier, both posts, crossbar and back of the net using the actual perspective projection; central chances retain a tight crop, while wide positions ease wider only as required. Attack direction comes from current possession or an active shot and respects second-half end changes and local two-player possession.

Pan remains eased. Contextual widening is limited to 0.65 zoom units per second; ordinary zoom changes and narrowing retain the 0.16 limit. Wind-up/contact still freeze zoom. If a chance arrives suddenly, ball visibility takes priority while widening catches up, so the whole net may take a moment to enter the frame. Portrait end-line bounds reserve room for the elevated goal frame rather than clipping it under the HUD.

Four added checks cover both flanks/goals/halves, desktop and portrait, full goal and carrier bounds, approach camera movement, contact locking, immediate wide-position shots, and UI selection of the attacking goal without requiring a drag. Existing projected pointer, scoring and replay tests remain in place. These are projection/DOM checks, not a claim of independently verified live camera feel.

When the goal is still outside the usable viewport at shooting range, a compact **GOAL AIM** overlay supplies an immediate fallback. The two post dots lie exactly on the current projected rays to the real posts; their connecting segment is the aiming window. A green dot shows the current shot intersection, and an amber dot plus WIDE label identifies an attempt outside the posts. This is directional guidance, not a second goal or a guaranteed scoring zone; defenders and keepers still affect the shot. It disappears when the actual goal frame clears the HUD and screen edges, on loss of player possession, and outside live play.

Three additional checks exercise the very first SHOT-capable frame without any camera settlement; every SHOT frame across a 131-frame (2.18-second) held approach in both halves and desktop/portrait layouts; real pointer release and single contact; exact post-ray geometry and between-post targeting; wide attempts; local sides; and replay/throw-in/opponent-possession suppression. They evaluate the production event handlers and projection math in the DOM harness. The overlay's live visual legibility still needs independent browser review. This follow-up does not accelerate the camera or change shot/keeper physics.

## Shot contact and aiming follow-up

Fast shots now use the same eight-unit body-contact radius for outfield players on both teams. Previously team-mates retained the nineteen-unit slow-ball receiving radius, which reproduced an immediate possession transfer beside the launch path during the contact frame. A directly intersecting team-mate or defender can still block the shot; this change adds no blanket launch immunity and leaves keeper handling and slow-pass control intact. This defect was reproduced in the current baseline; its introduction date has not been established.

Pointer shot/pass intent now persists from preview through queued wind-up and contact. A wide-angle SHOT could previously become a pass because the projected target beyond the goal line exceeded the engine's separate target-coordinate heuristic. Intent is explicit now. Very wide rays outside the supported goal-sector window still preview and release as passes; teammate-target assistance remains a pass. Green guidance means the aiming ray crosses between the posts, not that a goal is guaranteed.

Guidance markers have dark contrast discs and a thicker backing; the opaque label chooses the least-overlapping location among nearby candidates while post positions remain on their exact aiming rays.

Five additional checks cover near-launch team-mate capture at 30/60/120 Hz for both teams and halves; real body blocks by either team versus adjacent non-contact; pointer-generated goals, keeper saves, misses and completed teammate passes in both halves on desktop/portrait; wide-ray pass consistency; and guide label placement/contrast geometry. The two bug-specific tests fail on the prior source and pass after the fix. Live readability still needs independent browser review.

## Focused offside and goalkeeper audit

This iteration prioritises actual involvement before a touch. Offside decisions now pass through the normal indirect-free-kick flow with a visible OFFSIDE notice, offence marker and accessible explanation. The prior reception checks and restart exceptions remain. Tests cover both teams, both halves and 30/60/120 Hz; passive/retreating attackers, clear sight lines, wide/high balls and positions behind the keeper remain negative controls. An integration test runs a real shot into the obstruction decision, then sets up and takes the resulting free kick.

The keeper's existing eight-second sanction now has a final-five-second visual/accessibility cue, consistent with [Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/). Backpasses and direct teammate throws still use feet automatically: the game does not expose deliberate illegal handling for adjudication. Catch-only saves mean there is no parry/rebound to classify. No new handling offence or card system is claimed. The limited late-challenge shot advantage described below was added separately.

Cards and general advantage were reviewed against [Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/) and [Law 5](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-referee/). Cards and possession-based advantage remain deferred. The contact model classifies specific careless challenges, without reliable reckless/excessive-force or DOGSO classification. Only the bounded late-challenge shot trial described below is implemented. The pause-screen rules note reflects this scope.

All eight added checks pass alongside the previous suite. Six seeded AI matches reached full time (four results, two level matches entering shootouts) without a stuck state. Those samples generated no new involvement calls; the new rules are demonstrated by deterministic scenarios, not claimed as independently verified in live play.

## Honest shot forecast and limited advantage

The old pointer marker was a ground target twenty world units beyond the goal. It did not represent the whole-ball crossing plane or crossbar clearance. The replacement traces the same launch state, friction, gravity, bounce and first-boundary calculation used by actual play. A dashed path and goal-plane marker report LOW, LOFTED, WIDE, HIGH, SHORT or TIGHT MARGIN with **IF CLEAR** feedback. Post margins include the ball radius; crossing height includes the rendering height scale. Ground and lofted forecasts use the app's 60 Hz flight step. Different simulation steps can alter airborne estimates slightly, so near-frame placements are marked conservatively.

Pointer preparation, locked wind-up and early/live shot flight share this forecast. The world target remains stable after release even when the camera moves. Saves, blocks and subsequent possession changes are not forecast: an on-target indication does not guarantee a goal. Ordinary pointer shots are grounded, and the reported live miss has not been established as a height or scoring-rule defect. Synthetic airborne tests verify high-miss feedback without claiming ordinary pointer input offers height control.

For a detected careless late challenge, a clearly goal-bound shot already in flight can continue under an explicit advantage signal. The original offence, location and restart are retained. A goal satisfies the advantage; otherwise its next touch, exit or two-second expiry recalls the original free kick or penalty exactly once. A shot already projected wide or high gets the foul immediately. Pause freezes the trial. This is intentionally limited to finishing an imminent existing shot: broader possession-based advantage, successive offences, disciplinary severity, cards and full simultaneous-offence judgement remain outside scope.

Eight added tests cover actual crossing coordinates/heights at matching 30/60/120 Hz steps, both teams/halves, post margins, airborne misses, moving-camera pointer release, a real defensive block despite an on-target forecast, and genuine late-challenge detection followed by goals, saves/blocks, recalls, timeout and accessible pause/restart flows. Six seeded AI matches reached full time without getting stuck; they produced no advantage trials, so new advantage behavior is evidenced by deterministic match scenarios rather than sampled natural AI play. Live visual legibility still requires independent browser QA.

## Verification

The committed simulation/DOM suite has 181 checks. New coverage includes charging/late-contact fouls for both teams and halves, clean-tackle controls, 30/60/120 Hz contact classification, direct-kick positioning/scoring, penalty keeper/arc positioning, backward rejection, actual penalty flight, saves, second touches, pause/resume and interval/full-time completion. It retains all earlier restart, boundary, offside and input tests. Added UI sequences move an already-held pointer through recovery and camera movement before releasing once, and simulate an actual goal-line miss through automatic goal-kick setup and held-pointer contact in desktop/portrait viewports.

Six seeded full-match simulations still finish normally with goals, saves and interceptions. The suite also checks shooting outcomes at 30/60/120 Hz, near/mid/long-range placement, both ends and halves, projected mouse/touch sequences, cancelled input, planted contact, smooth goal framing and full-team scoring approaches. These small simulations do not establish human difficulty or visual polish.

This revision was tested by evaluating the real engine and UI event handlers in a V8 DOM/canvas harness. Browser plugin and executor are unavailable in this delegated thread.
